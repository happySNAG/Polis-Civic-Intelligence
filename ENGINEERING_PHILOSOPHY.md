# Engineering Philosophy

> Companion to the [README](README.md) and [Project Summary](PROJECT_SUMMARY.md).

Polis is built around a simple premise: public information is only useful when residents can find it, understand it, and verify it.

## Trust before speed

Publishing quickly is not valuable when evidence, freshness, or context is missing.

## Sources before opinions

Important claims should remain traceable to their evidence.

## Explain uncertainty

The system should communicate what is known, what is inferred, what is stale, and what remains unresolved.

## Governance before automation

Data acquisition, normalization, review, correction, and publication require explicit rules and accountable boundaries.

## Participation before polarization

Civic technology should help people learn, attend, volunteer, contact representatives, and contribute locally.

## Privacy before engagement

A resident's civic record is not a leaderboard, social score, streak, or public identity. It belongs to the resident.

## Synthetic first

Development and testing should avoid presenting invented civic information as fact. Live data belongs behind governance and verification.

## Human judgment before automation

Automation can organize evidence and support review, but it should not conceal consequential editorial or civic decisions.

## Local before national

Civic understanding begins where decisions are closest to daily life.
